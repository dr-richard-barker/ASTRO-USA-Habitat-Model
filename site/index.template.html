The habitat

What the Food Production Module holds, as described by the ASTRO-USA team.

ASTRO-USA (Analog Simulation Training and Research Outpost Utilizing Self-sustaining Architecture) is an analog habitat being developed at Purdue University. Its first physical module, the Food Production Module (FPM), converts a shipping container (modelled as a 40 ft high-cube) into a growing space with a vertical nutrient-film (NFT) hydroponic wall, a propagation rack, Kratky and Dutch-bucket systems, a bay for bioreactor payloads, a battery bank and a work space, with the HVAC unit in an attic at the far end. It is designed to support a crew of {{MissionCrew}} for a two-week mission (Peters et al., ICES-2026-251).

Analysis results

Airflow, heat and CO₂ from OpenFOAM, plus well-mixed models where they suffice.

Every simulation is a time-averaged transient run that must close its mass, heat, CO₂, water-vapour and age-of-air balances within 1 % and be statistically stationary before any value is used.

Validation: choosing the turbulence model

Against laser-Doppler measurements of the IEA Annex 20 ventilated-room benchmark, the standard k-ε model erred by {{KeLo}}–{{KeHi}} of the inlet velocity and SST k-ω by {{SstLo}}–{{SstHi}}, so the habitat runs use k-ε.

The baseline airflow

The cold supply jet leaves the attic partition at ceiling height and falls to the floor part-way along the room; the two crew plumes are the warmest, highest-CO₂ air. The run closes its heat balance to {{BaseHeatErr}} % and its CO₂ balance to {{BaseCOTwoErr}} %.

Room-wide averages are robust: they change by at most {{GciDomainMax}} % across three meshes and {{ModelDomainMax}} % between turbulence models. Single-point values are not (up to {{GciLocalMax}} and {{ModelLocalMax}} %).

Mesh study ({{GciNOk}} of {{GciN}} quantities can be given a GCI)

Celik et al. (2008) procedure on {{GciHFine}}, {{GciHMed}} and {{GciHCoarse}} mm meshes. A GCI is quoted only where the mesh-to-mesh differences exceed the time-averaging noise and the observed order p lies between {{PRangeLo}} and {{PRangeHi}}.

Occupancy and airflow

With six crew the room's CO₂ rise grows ×{{StwoCOTwoRatio}}. Halving the airflow raises the temperature rise ×{{SfiveaTRatio}}, CO₂ ×{{SfiveaCOTwoRatio}} and the age of air ×{{SfiveaAgeRatio}}, more than the ×2 of a perfectly mixed room: the air-change efficiency falls from {{BaseACE}} to {{SfiveaACE}}. The room mixes less well at low flow, not only less often, so the attic unit's airflow matters.

Where bioreactor off-gas should go

At the placeholder rate the bioreactor rack's CO₂ adds about {{OffgasMixed}} ppm mixed through the room, against about {{BaseReturnCOTwo}} ppm from two working crew; even ducted to the hydroponic wall it raises the canopy by only {{OffgasCanopyNFT}} ppm. At these rates it is neither a hazard nor a useful CO₂ supply for the plants.

If the HVAC fails

With the door shut and no air exchange, CO₂ reaches OSHA's limit (an eight-hour average) in about {{SthreeSixCrew}} h with six crew and {{SthreeTwoCrew}} h with two. Leakage slows this a lot, so a door-shut CO₂ decay test is the measurement that settles it.

Concepts

The ASTRO-USA plan, and how this model is built.

The Mini-Hab and the primary habitat

In the ASTRO-USA plan the Food Production Module is one of several modules of a secondary habitat, the Mini-Hab, alongside a Biosafety Level 2 Laboratory Module, Crew Quarters and NASA Kennedy Space Center's BioWaTERR wastewater-treatment trailer, which together aim to close the waste and nutrient loops; SIMOC Live and an Opto 22 system will monitor and control them. A primary habitat is envisaged as modular cylindrical and spherical sections in aluminium or titanium. Only the FPM is dimensioned in the paper; the other modules here are concept-level placeholders.

The generated concept encloses {{PrimaryVol}} m³ of pressurised volume (airlock excluded) for a crew of {{PrimaryCrew}}; it meets NASA's minimum net habitable volume of {{NHVGuide}} m³ per person (Whitmire et al. 2014) once at least {{PrimaryMinFrac}} of that volume is habitable.

One ledger, three models

Every dimension and load is a ledger record with its value, unit and either a source with a verbatim quote or a declared assumption with its reason; the build refuses anything else. A parts registry turns the ledger into three models, each with its own checks, so that replacing an assumption with a measured fact updates all of them.

What the team can supply next

{{NAskTeam}} assumptions can only be replaced by the ASTRO-USA team. The answers that unblock the most:

  1. Grow lights: fixture, count, wattage, photoperiod, and whether they are inside the power budget.
  2. Attic HVAC: make and model, airflow, grille positions, and whether it brings in outdoor air.
  3. Container leakage: a door-shut CO₂ decay measurement.
  4. The CAD model behind the paper's renders, to check this geometry against it.
  5. Bioreactor payloads: which hardware, with envelopes, gas ports and loads.
